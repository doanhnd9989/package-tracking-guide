# Your Parcel Arrived in Your Country — Who Delivers It Now?

Your tracking says something like **"Arrived at transit node, awaiting distribution, RO"** — and then
nothing. This guide explains what that scan means, which company has the parcel now, whether you get
a second tracking number, and how long the rest actually takes.

All figures below are measured on delivered parcels tracked on the 24hTrack platform (120 days to
2026-09-24) and are published as CC BY 4.0 data in [`data/final-leg-by-carrier.csv`](../data/final-leg-by-carrier.csv).

## 1. "Arrived at transit node" is a building, not a van

The parcel came off an aircraft, cleared import, and was trucked to a sorting facility — usually near
the airport of entry rather than near you. "Awaiting distribution" means it is in a pile waiting to be
sorted by postcode.

It is good news. It is deliberately **not** an address, **not** a city, and **not** a promise about
tomorrow. The scan is generated by the facility, and facilities do not publish their own postcode.

## 2. One order, two jobs, sometimes two numbers

| | The export number | The local number |
|---|---|---|
| Who issues it | the seller / the exporter | the courier in your country |
| What it covers | pickup, export scan, flight, customs, arrival | depot, sorting, van, your door |
| Roughly how long | most of the calendar | the last day or two |
| Where you got it | printed on your order | your order page, if at all |

A concrete pair from the data: an export-side operator like FXYL runs a median of **22.8 days** from
first carrier scan to delivery, while a last-mile carrier such as YWE (United States) delivers half of
its parcels within about **2 days** of its own first scan. Same journeys, two completely different clocks.

## 3. Does the original number keep updating after the handover?

Usually yes — and the scan counts show it:

| Shipment type | Median scans published |
|---|---|
| 4PX (cross-border) | 20 |
| Yanwen Express (cross-border) | 19 |
| China Post · Cainiao | 18 |
| FXYL (cross-border) | 17 |
| USPS (domestic) | 7 |

A cross-border number publishes two to three times as many events as a domestic one. Those extra
events are largely the **destination** side of the trip, still reported under the number the seller
gave you. A quiet few days around the handover is a gap, not an ending.

## 4. Where a second tracking number comes from

If one exists, it is created by the local courier **after** they take custody. That has three
consequences people waste a lot of effort on:

- The exporter usually cannot give it to you. It did not exist when they shipped.
- It appears in your marketplace order page or an email from the shop, not from the carrier.
- If it never appears, that is normal. In most networks the original number is already showing you
  the same scans.

## 5. From the last transit scan to the door

Once you see that final transit scan, the remaining wait is short:

| Carrier | Median | 80% within | 90% within |
|---|---|---|---|
| USPS | ~5 hours | ~8 hours | ~10 hours |
| Yanwen Express | ~8 hours | ~12 hours | ~34 hours |
| China Post | ~8 hours | ~46 hours | ~84 hours |
| Cainiao | ~8 hours | ~31 hours | ~91 hours |
| 4PX | ~8 hours | ~17 hours | ~58 hours |
| FXYL | ~1 day | ~26 hours | ~29 hours |
| YunExpress | ~26 hours | ~79 hours | ~96 hours |

The waiting happens **before** that scan — in customs queues and sorting backlogs — not after it.

## 6. When the handover has actually gone wrong

Four patterns are worth acting on:

1. **10+ days quiet after a scan inside your own country.** The last leg is short; it should not stall.
2. **Any wording about duties owed or documents required.** Nothing moves until you act, and nobody
   will chase you.
3. **Return to sender · refused · failed delivery.** The clock is suddenly short.
4. **Message the seller, not the courier.** You are not the courier's customer. The seller bought the
   shipping and is the only party who can open a claim on it.

## FAQ

**Which local courier will deliver it?**
Whoever the exporter contracted in your country — often the national post, sometimes a private courier.
It is usually named in the last few scans rather than at the start, so on day one there is no honest
answer to give you.

**Will I get a Romanian / German / British tracking number?**
Only if the local courier issues one, and only through your order page. Ask the shop, not the exporter.

**It has been in "awaiting distribution" for three days. Is it lost?**
Almost certainly not. Across delivered parcels, the single longest silent stretch runs to a median of
about four days, and more than a quarter go a full week with no update before arriving.

**Can I ask the courier to deliver it faster or to a different address?**
Not as the recipient of a cross-border shipment. The shipping contract belongs to the seller.
