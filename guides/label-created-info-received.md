# "Label Created" / "Info Received": What It Means and How Long It Lasts

**Short answer:** "Label Created", "Info Received", "Shipping information received" and "Pre-shipment" all mean the carrier has the parcel's *data*, not the parcel. The shop printed a label and sent the details electronically. The first real scan appears only when a person or machine physically handles the box — usually within 1–3 days.

## Why a parcel sits on this status

1. **The label was printed before the parcel was packed.** The label can exist days before the box does.
2. **Pickup runs on a schedule.** Weekends and public holidays add a day or two.
3. **No individual acceptance scan.** Parcels dropped in a mailbox or handed over in a stack often get their first scan at the sorting facility instead of at drop-off.
4. **Cross-border parcels wait for a batch.** Consolidators often fill a shipment before anything leaves, and nothing is scanned until it does.

## Does "Info Received" mean the parcel is in my country?

Not necessarily. The status often names the company that **will** deliver the parcel — for cross-border shipments that is frequently your local courier — while the parcel is still in a warehouse abroad. Location only starts with the first physical scan.

## When to act

- **Domestic:** about 7 days with no new scan → contact the sender.
- **Cross-border:** about 10 days with no new scan → contact the sender.
- Ask one question: *"Has it been handed to the carrier, and is there an acceptance receipt?"* The carrier cannot help with a parcel it has not received.
- If you bought on a marketplace, note the buyer-protection deadline.

## FAQ

**Is "Label Created" a sign of a scam?** No. It is the normal first step for nearly every parcel.

**Can the carrier speed it up?** No — it does not have the parcel yet.
