# The Driver Wants Cash: how to tell a real cash-on-delivery parcel from a scam

A courier is at the door and will not hand over the box until you pay. In large parts of the world that is completely routine. It is also exactly what doorstep payment fraud imitates. This guide is how to tell them apart in about ten seconds, and what to do when the numbers do not match.

## The short version

**The amount is in your order confirmation, not on the tracking page.** A real cash-on-delivery order states the figure in the order, in your currency. Match that against what is being asked. No matching order means no payment. Pay the courier in cash or by card at the door — never by transfer, payment link or QR code.

## What cash on delivery actually is

Cash on delivery, usually written COD, means the seller offered pay-when-it-arrives at checkout and the courier collects that payment on the seller's behalf. The courier is acting as a cashier, not charging you a delivery fee: the money goes back to the shop.

It is a product couriers sell to merchants, not a property of a particular parcel. GFS Express — one of the networks that carries Chinese e-commerce orders into the Gulf — advertises cash collection with weekly payouts to the merchant, and lists local operations in Saudi Arabia, Bahrain, Jordan, Kuwait, Oman, Qatar, Azerbaijan, Georgia and South Africa.

That is also roughly the map of where COD is normal. It is unusual in the UK and the US, and ordinary across the Gulf, much of Africa and a lot of South-East Asia. If you live in one of those markets, a driver asking for money is not by itself a warning sign.

## Four signs it is not your parcel

1. **You did not order anything, and nobody will say what is inside.** A courier delivering a real order can tell you the sender.
2. **The amount matches no order you placed.** Not roughly — exactly. A genuine COD figure is the price of something you chose.
3. **They want a transfer, a payment link or a QR code.** Legitimate collection happens at the door, to the courier, in cash or by card. An off-channel payment is the move the scam depends on.
4. **No receipt, no company name, and a lot of hurry.** A real courier can wait a minute while you open your email. Someone running a scam cannot, because the whole thing relies on you deciding at the door.

Ask for a receipt either way. A courier collecting properly has one, on paper or as a line in an app.

## Customs duty is a different bill

Do not confuse COD with import charges. Duty and VAT are charged by the carrier or the post office, usually reach you **before** delivery by SMS or email with a link, and can land on an order you already paid for in full.

- **COD** = the price of the goods, collected for the seller, at the door.
- **Duty** = tax and a handling fee, billed by the carrier, usually in advance.

Both can exist on the same parcel. Neither is a sign of the other being fake.

## What tracking can and cannot tell you

Tracking carries scans: a status, a time, often a place. It does not carry prices. **No tracking site can tell you what you owe**, because carriers do not publish that figure — it lives in the order record. Any page that shows you an amount is reading it from somewhere other than the carrier.

What tracking is genuinely good for here is confirming that a real parcel exists and is where the person at your door claims it is. Paste the number before delivery day: if the scans show it reaching your country, clearing customs and going out for delivery, something real is on its way.

## If the amount is wrong, refuse

Refusing is reversible. The tracking will show a failed delivery attempt and then a return, the seller will see it, and your money is still yours. Paying first and arguing afterwards is the version that is hard to undo.

Then message the **seller**, not the courier. The seller set the payment method and holds the shipping contract.

## For sellers

Most "is this a scam?" tickets in COD markets are preventable with three lines of copy:

1. State the COD amount in the order confirmation, in the buyer's currency, on its own line.
2. Say once how collection works: cash or card to the courier, never a link.
3. Separate duty from COD in your messaging, so a buyer who already paid does not think they are being charged twice.
