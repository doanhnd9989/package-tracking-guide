# Why You Now Pay Duty on a Cheap Parcel (US, 2026)

A low-value parcel from overseas reaches your country and, instead of arriving, it asks you for money. Nothing has gone wrong and it is not a scam text. The rule that used to let cheap parcels into the United States duty free has been withdrawn.

*Checked October 2026. Customs rules move — treat the dates below as something to re-verify, not to memorise.*

## Short answer

The United States suspended duty-free **de minimis** treatment for all countries on **29 August 2025**, and in **June 2026** CBP made that suspension indefinite. Shipments valued at **$800 or less** now require a customs entry (informal or formal), and duty can be due on them. If the seller shipped **DDP** (delivered duty paid) they settled it in advance and you should not be asked for anything. If they did not, the carrier pays customs on your behalf and collects it back from you — usually with a handling fee — before releasing the parcel. Genuine **gifts valued at $100 or less remain exempt**.

## What changed

| | Before | Now |
|---|---|---|
| Low-value threshold | $800 or less entered duty free with minimal paperwork | Suspended for all countries (29 Aug 2025), indefinite since June 2026 |
| Entry process | Entry **Type 86**, a simplified low-value entry | Type 86 no longer accepted; an informal or formal entry is required |
| Who is billed | Usually nobody | The importer of record — the seller if DDP, otherwise you |
| Gifts | Exempt | Still exempt at $100 or less |

If a shipping company's website still advertises "T86 clearance", that page has not been updated. Several cross-border carriers' marketing pages still carry it.

## Why the bill lands on you

Duty is owed on the goods, and there are only two candidates to pay it:

- **The seller, before shipping.** This is DDP — delivered duty paid. The cost is built into what you already paid, and nobody contacts you.
- **You, before delivery.** The carrier advances the duty to customs, then collects it from you, normally with its own handling or disbursement fee on top.

The deciding factor is how the seller chose to ship, not the value of your order or the carrier's goodwill. If you are being asked to pay, the useful question to the seller is simply: *did you ship this DDP?*

## How a customs hold looks in tracking

It does not announce itself. While a parcel waits for an entry to be filed, or for a charge to be paid, nobody is physically handling it — so nobody scans it, and the tracking page simply stops moving for days.

Before assuming the parcel is lost, read the **last scan** rather than the silence after it:

- `Customs clearance`, `Import clearance`, `Held by customs`, `Awaiting payment` — the clock is running on paperwork, not on a van.
- Parcels in that state usually start moving again on their own once the entry clears or the charge is paid.
- A silence that begins *before* the parcel ever left the origin country is a different problem — that is usually a label created but never handed over.

## Scam messages look similar — how to tell them apart

Fake "pay a customs fee" texts rose alongside the rule change. A genuine request:

- comes from the carrier that is actually delivering your parcel, and names the tracking number you recognise;
- can be verified by going to the carrier's own site yourself rather than following the link in the message;
- asks for a duty or tax amount, not for card details "to verify your identity".

Check your tracking page first. If there is no customs scan on it, be careful with the message.

## This is United States policy

Every country sets its own threshold and its own rules. The EU removed its low-value VAT exemption in 2021; the UK, Australia and others each have their own arrangements. What applies to your parcel depends on **where it is going**, not where it came from.
