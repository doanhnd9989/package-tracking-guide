# The carrier paused its service — your number will never scan

*Part of the [Package Tracking Guide](../README.md).*

A tracking number that has never moved is usually read as "the parcel is lost" or "the number is wrong". There is a third explanation that almost nobody checks, and ruling it in or out takes about a minute: **the company carrying it has suspended the service.**

## Why this happens more often than you would think

Cross-border parcels are rarely carried end to end by one company. Most start with a **consolidator** — a firm that gathers parcels in one country, clears them for export, flies them, clears them on arrival, and hands them to a delivery company where you live. Buyers normally never hear of these firms, because they are hired by sellers, not by shoppers.

Consolidators suspend lanes. A partner stops flying, a customs rule changes, the economics stop working, and the service goes quiet.

The paperwork does not go quiet with it. A consignment number issued the week before a pause still exists, still passes any format check, and will simply never collect a scan.

Operators that pause usually say so on their own website — sometimes alongside a line stating that the site is not a courier company and is not responsible for parcel handling. It is rarely hidden. It is just that nobody thinks to look there.

## The fact that makes it make sense

A tracking scan is **not** created when you pay, and **not** when a label is printed.

It is created when physical goods are booked into a warehouse and somebody reads a barcode.

No booking in, no scan. So a tracking record has two separate lives: the **number**, which exists the moment a seller generates it, and the **shipment**, which exists only once something physical is handed over. Most people assume the first implies the second. It does not — and that gap is exactly where a paused lane hides.

Your number can be completely real while your parcel is still sitting with the seller.

## How to check, in about a minute

1. **Open the carrier's own website** — not a third-party tracking page, ours included. Look at the home page and the about page for a notice. Firms that pause a service generally say so in plain words.
2. **Check whether any scan ever landed.** This distinction matters and most tracking pages blur it:
   - **Never scanned at all** → a booking problem. The goods have probably not been handed over.
   - **Scanned, then stopped** → usually transit. Customs, a flight, a depot. Often just slow.
3. **Then talk to the seller, not the carrier.** If the lane has paused, no tracking site can restart it. The seller booked the shipment, so the seller can see whether it was handed over — and the seller is the only party who can rebook it on a service that is still running.

## What to ask the seller

Not "where is my order" — that is vague enough to be ignored. Ask for two specific things:

1. **Confirmation that the parcel was physically handed over, with a date.**
2. **If it was not — a new consignment number on a service that is operating.**

Sellers answer that far faster, because it is specific and they can look it up in their own dashboard in seconds.

## The takeaway

A blank tracking page is information. It is just not the information most people assume.

It usually does not mean "your parcel vanished somewhere over an ocean". Far more often it means "nothing has physically started yet" — which is a better situation than the one you were imagining, because the parcel is not lost. It has not left.
