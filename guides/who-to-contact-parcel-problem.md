# Seller, Carrier or Tracking Site: Who Actually Fixes a Parcel Problem

You ring the courier about a late parcel and they tell you to contact the seller. It sounds
like a brush-off. It isn't. It's a consequence of who paid for the shipping — and once you
know the rule, you stop losing days on the wrong company.

## The three parties

| Who | What they did | What they can do for you |
|---|---|---|
| **The seller / shop** | Took your money, chose the courier, bought the label | Open an investigation, resend, refund. **Almost every remedy lives here.** |
| **The carrier** | Was paid by the seller — not by you — to move the box | Move it, scan it. Usually will not open a claim for a recipient. |
| **A tracking site** | Reads the scans both of them publish | Show you the timeline. Holds no parcel, no money, no contract. |

You are the carrier's *recipient*, not its customer. The shipping contract, the insurance and
the right to claim sit with whoever bought the label. In the EU this is also the legal default:
under Article 20 of the Consumer Rights Directive, where the trader arranges carriage, the risk
of loss or damage stays with the trader until the consumer takes physical possession.

## The one question that sorts it

**Has the parcel been scanned at all?**

- **No first scan.** The seller still physically has it. No courier can help with a box they
  have never touched. → Seller.
- **Moving abroad, or in customs.** Nobody is "holding" it; nothing is being handled, so
  nothing is scanned. → Wait. See [tracking-not-updating](tracking-not-updating.md).
- **Scanned inside your own country, then quiet 10+ days.** This is the pause worth chasing.
  → Ask the seller to open a case with the carrier.
- **Says delivered, but it isn't there.** → Seller. Only the sender can request proof of
  delivery. See [package-delivered-but-not-received](package-delivered-but-not-received.md).

## Four phrases that need you, today

Most tracking wording means "wait". These four mean act:

- **Duties or taxes due** — nothing moves until it's paid.
- **Documents required** — customs is waiting on you, not on the carrier.
- **Address incorrect / insufficient** — fix it with the seller immediately.
- **Held for collection** — these expire, often in 1–2 weeks, then go back to sender.

## The handover makes this more confusing than it should be

On a cross-border order the company named on your tracking often handles only the first leg.
Near the end a local operator finishes the job, sometimes under its own separate reference.
That's why the page can stop while the parcel keeps moving — and why asking the original
carrier about that leg gets you nowhere. See
[parcel-arrived-in-your-country](parcel-arrived-in-your-country.md).

## What to write so you get a real answer

Bring a number, not a feeling. Include:

1. The tracking number.
2. The last scan — its **date and place**, quoted.
3. How many days it has been quiet.
4. What you want: investigation, replacement, or refund.

> "Tracking LP00123456789CN hasn't moved since 14 September. Last scan was an arrival scan in
> my own country, so it's been 11 days inside the destination network. Please open an
> investigation with the carrier."

That gets a reply. "Where is my order" gets a template.

## Marketplace escalation

If the seller stops responding, the marketplace — not the courier — is the next step. Every
major platform has a buyer-protection window that runs from the order or estimated-delivery
date, and it **expires**. Open the case before it closes; you can always withdraw it if the
parcel turns up. Check the exact window on your own order page, since it varies by platform
and by item.
